Information retrieval is the task of finding material, usually documents, that satisfies an information need from within a large collection. A user expresses the need as a query, and the system returns the documents it believes are relevant.

Early retrieval systems were built for librarians and legal researchers who wrote precise boolean queries. Modern search engines hide most of that machinery, but the core ideas are the same: process the documents once, build an index, and answer queries quickly against it.
